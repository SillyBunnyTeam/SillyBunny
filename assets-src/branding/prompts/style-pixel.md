Pixel art style: detailed pixel art in the spirit of late 16-bit console games, drawn on a strict square pixel grid.
Outlines: coloured outlines in the darkest shade of the local colour, never black; selective outlining where shapes meet.
Palette: a small locked palette of 24 to 32 colours; hue-shifted ramps, with shadows shifting toward violet or blue and highlights shifting toward warm yellow.
Clusters: clean pixel clusters, no orphan pixels, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no sub-pixel detail; dithering only for large gradients.
Sticker edge (an important trait): an unbroken white outer outline one grid pixel wide around the whole silhouette, including every hair strand, both rabbit ears and the tail, with a soft lavender-grey rim one grid pixel wide outside it.
Proportions: the same character proportions as the illustrated design sheet, simplified for the grid, with a larger head ratio at 64x64 and below.
Rabbit ears: the ears are a primary means of expression and are drawn with clearly different silhouettes per emotion: standing straight up when alert, bent at the middle when relaxed, drooping backwards when sad, splayed or shooting up when surprised, tilted when curious.
No human ears: the only ears are the rabbit ears on top of the head; the sides of the head where human ears would be are covered by hair.
